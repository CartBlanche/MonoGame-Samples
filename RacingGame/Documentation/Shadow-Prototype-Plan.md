# RacingGame shadow prototype: scope and plan

Branch: `racinggame-shadows`, created from `racinggame-lighting` (so it builds on the time of day, per-pixel lights and the rig). Desktop (DesktopGL, macOS) only, Release builds only, no Android/Windows/iOS builds. Same working rules as `Lighting-Plan.md` section 9 (caps, stall rule, at most 2 agents at once, no commits by agents, one-line commit messages, no em dash).

This is a **prototype with kill criteria**, not a commitment. Its purpose is to answer one question with measurements and your sign-off on the look: *would shadows applied inside the lighting shader be better than the original screen-space shadow overlay, at no worse cost?*

---

## 1. Why consider it

The original shadow pipeline darkens the **finished picture**. That causes real, visible limits, several of which the lighting work has already had to work around:

- Shadows multiply everything the receive pass drew, so a shadow also dims **headlight pools, lamp light, and emissive glow** that fall inside it. I had to fade the shadow colour out at night and in tunnels (`TrackLighting.ShadowColor`, tunnel blend) as a workaround.
- Two extra full-screen steps per frame (a screen-sized receive pass that re-draws the terrain and nearby objects, then a blur pass and a multiply composite): 6 render target sets per frame with glow off.
- The blur is screen space, so shadow edges can halo or smear across depth discontinuities.
- Shadow strength is one global colour (0.25, 0.26, 0.27); it cannot follow the sun term only.

Shadows applied in the lit shaders only dim the **sun/moon** term, so lights stay bright, and the receive pass, the blur and the composite can go away.

What this prototype is **not**: headlight or lamp shadows (separate, costly, later), a PBR change. (Earlier drafts said you wanted hard-edged shadows; that was a misreading, corrected 2026-11-23: the goal is **natural, environment-driven shadows**, see the revised goal at the end of this document.)

## 2. How shadows work today (read from the code, 2026-11-23)

Per frame, from `GameScreen.Render` and `ShadowMapShader.PrepareGameShadows` (`Shaders/ShadowMapShader.cs:398-420`):

1. **Generate** (`GenerateShadows`, `:285-320`): casters (the road, objects near the track, the car) are drawn into a depth map from a virtual **perspective** light placed `virtualLightDistance` (171.6) behind the visible area, looking at a point in front of the car; field of view `atan2(virtualVisibleRange 129.25, 171.6)`, near 1, far 218.75 (`:234-262`, `SetParameters` `:195-215`). The target is `RenderToTexture.SizeType.ShadowMap`: **2048x2048** with High Detail, 1024 otherwise (`RenderToTexture.cs:105-115`); depth is linear `w`, stored in a `Single` target. Technique `GenerateShadowMap20` (`Content/Shaders/ShadowMap.fx`).
2. **Receive** (`RenderShadows`, `:362-390`): the receivers (terrain, nearby objects when High Detail, the car) are re-drawn into a **screen-sized** target with technique `UseShadowMap20` (`ShadowMap.fx:178-240`): 10 Poisson taps over the shadow map, compare `depth - depthBias(0.00065)`, multiplied by a **distance fade texture** (`ShadowDistanceFadeoutMap`), result `lerp(1, ShadowColor, amount)`. Banners and signs do not receive.
3. **Blur** (`ShadowMapBlur.RenderShadows`, `Shaders/ShadowMapBlur.cs:119-138` and `:142+`): the receive target is blurred (`PostScreenShadowBlur.fx`) into a second target.
4. **Composite** (`ShowShadows`, `ShadowMapBlur.cs:193+`, called from `GameScreen.Render` after the 3D scene): the blurred map is multiplied over the frame (`ZeroSourceBlend`: dest = dest x src).

So the existing look is **already filtered** (10 taps plus a blur), not razor hard. Matching that edge character is part of the acceptance test.

## 3. What TPS does (read from its code; not seen rendered here)

`ThirdPersonShooter/Core/Rendering/ShadowMap.cs`, `SpotShadows.cs` and `Core/Content/Effects/PbrCommon.fxh` (`ShadowFactor`, lines 96-118): the shadow lookup happens **inside the lit shader** and only scales the key light; the receiver position is moved along its normal and the depth bias scales with the surface slope (less acne and peter-panning); the map is texel-snapped to the camera; a 3x3 filter; an edge fade at the map border; a separate atlas for spot lights. Its map is orthographic with a 32 m radius around the player, which is why it is **not directly usable** for a racing game that needs far more range; RacingGame's perspective map already covers about 258 units across at 2048 texels (about 0.126 units per texel). The idea we borrow is the **in-shader lookup on the sun term plus normal-offset and slope bias**, not the map layout.

## 4. Design for RacingGame

Keep the **generate pass exactly as it is** (it is validated and you liked its look). Replace steps 2 to 4 by a lookup in the lit shaders:

- **Shared include `Content/Shaders/TrackShadows.fxh`** (new; pattern of `TrackLighting.fxh`): `ShadowMode` (0 = off, 1 = on; GL starts parameters at 0, so 0 must mean "classic or no shadow term"), `ShadowVP` (world to the map's texture space and linear depth, composed once per frame on the CPU from `lightViewMatrix`, `lightProjectionMatrix` and the existing texture bias), `ShadowParams` (texel size, bias, normal offset, strength), the `ShadowMap` sampler and the existing distance fade texture. One function `TL_Shadow(worldPos, N)` returns 0..1 (1 = lit), 10 Poisson taps like the classic shader (a `ShadowTaps` option for fewer taps on Low).
- **Applied to the sun term only** in each lit technique: `bump * diffuseColor` becomes `bump * shadow * diffuseColor`, and the sun specular is scaled the same. Lamp and headlight terms (the per-pixel light loop from WS2) are not scaled. Ambient is not scaled (see the strength check in 6).
- **World position in every lit technique**, not only the per-pixel light path. WS2 already passes world position, normal and tangent as three extra interpolants when `LightingMode` is on; the review noted the legacy path may receive them anyway. The shadow lookup needs the world position in both paths, and one more interpolant (the shadow clip position) can be computed per vertex.
- **C# side** (`ShadowMapShader.cs`, a new `TrackLighting.Shadows.cs` partial): after `GenerateShadows`, publish `ShadowVP`, the map texture and the parameters to the lit effects with the same handle-caching pattern as `TrackLighting.EffectHandles` (the car and glass use the model's own effects, as WS2 found). When integrated shadows are on, **skip** the receive pass, the blur and `ShowShadows`.
- **Per-object receive flags:** classic excludes banners and signs and only handles objects near the track; the new path must keep banners and signs unshadowed (a per-material flag or a uniform set around those draws) and rely on the map bounds plus the fade texture for far objects.
- **Settings:** a prototype switch `RACINGGAME_SHADOWS=classic|integrated` for A/B. If adopted: `Lighting Off` stays on the classic path, so "Off = exactly the original game" remains true; Low and High use integrated shadows (Low with fewer taps).

## 6. Acceptance and kill criteria

All measured with the rig (fixed step, fixed seed, `RACINGGAME_FRAMES`), and always **looking at the images**, not only numbers.

1. **Classic untouched:** with `RACINGGAME_SHADOWS=classic` (and Lighting Off), frames match the baseline within the known noise (MAE under 0.15 on Advanced).
2. **Natural, and positioned like the original:** on every SP0 view, shadow positions and extents match classic where classic is right; shadows follow the environment (see the revised goal: fill from sky/ambient light, widening penumbra, one sun direction, time-of-day behaviour); no shimmering; you accept the look at the checkpoints. (The classic road-wide darkening is not reproduced unless you ask.)
3. **Strength check:** classic multiplies the finished pixel by about 0.25. Integrated darkens only the sun term, so the result depends on the ambient to sun ratio (landscape ambient 88 vs diffuse 234 at noon gives a shadow of roughly 27 to 35 percent of full brightness, close to classic by coincidence). Measure it; add a strength parameter if needed.
4. **Lights stay lit:** a headlight pool, lamp pool or tunnel lamp inside a shadow is as bright as outside it (this is the main thing classic cannot do).
5. **Cost no worse:** CPU Draw within +0.1 ms of classic; render target sets per frame down from 6 to 3 or fewer (glow off); fps with vsync off not worse; no new per-frame allocations. GPU time cannot be measured here (no Metal capture available to the agents), so the decision uses these proxies plus **your measurements on your machine**.
6. **No regressions:** Day with Lighting High matches the lighting branch frames when no shadows are involved; tunnels, glass, the ghost car and the menus unchanged.

**Kill criteria (stop and report, keep classic):** (a) the interpolator or instruction budget prevents converting the terrain or road shaders without breaking the look; (b) after 3 tuning iterations the edge character or strength cannot be brought within your acceptance; (c) the frame cost proxies are worse than classic; (d) the shadow map covers the road ahead worse than classic at racing speed.

**What you get at each stop:** after SP1/SP2 a working terrain and road shadow comparison; after SP3 everything converted behind a switch; after SP5 a decision document and, if it passes, a default switch. Failing the kill criteria leaves the game exactly as it is today.

## 7. Risks

- **Shader budget.** Every lit technique gets another lookup and at least one more interpolant; some reflection and car structs already use 7 TEXCOORDs (the review and WS2 measured this). Mitigation: prototype on the road and terrain first, count before converting more, a `ShadowTaps` option, and `ShadowMode` branches that keep the classic compile paths lean.
- **Per-object differences.** The car and glass use the model's own effects (WS2 had to plumb parameters there); banners and signs must not receive; objects far from the car must fade out. Mitigation: explicit receive flag, the existing fade texture, a checkpoint with these views.
- **Look drift.** You like the current look. Mitigation: the generate pass and the 10-tap filter are kept, classic stays selectable, you sign off at checkpoints.
- **GPU cost unknown.** Per-pixel lookups on every lit pixel, mostly terrain and road, versus two screen-sized passes saved. Mitigation: proxies, a tap-count option, and your Metal capture before adopting it as the default.
- **Interaction with glow:** the glow post effect (about +3.4 ms when on) is separate and unchanged; shadows no longer double-process through the glow's scene target differently, which should be checked once with glow on (the dev machine has it off in its settings file).
- **LAN and replays:** visual only; no protocol or physics change.

### SP1 + SP2 (2026-11-23): terrain and road, integrated shadows behind a switch. Uncommitted in worktree.

**Built:** `Content/Shaders/TrackShadows.fxh` (`ShadowMode`, `ShadowX/Y/W`, `ShadowParams`, `ShadowMap`, `shadowDistanceFadeoutTexture`, `TL_Shadow(P, N)` with the 10 literal Poisson taps, linear depth compare, bounds check, fade multiply; no extra filtering) included by `LandscapeNormalMapping.fx` (`DiffuseWithDetail20`) and `NormalMapping.fx`. In `NormalMapping.fx` the change is in `PS_Specular20` and `PS_SpecularRoad20` (road with and without High Detail). Only the sun term uses the shadow (`sunBump = bump * shadow`, diffuse and sun specular); ambient and `TL_Lights` are untouched. `Graphics/TrackLighting.Shadows.cs` (`RACINGGAME_SHADOWS=classic|integrated`, `TrackLighting.IntegratedShadows`, per-frame publish to `landscapeNormalMapping` and `normalMapping`, depth bias 0.00065, normal offset 0, strength 1). `ShadowMapShader.PrepareGameShadows` skips receive and blur when integrated, `GameScreen` skips `ShowShadows`.

**Findings (budget):** no new interpolators were needed: the WS2 world position, normal and tangent TEXCOORDs are already passed in the legacy path (terrain 6 TEXCOORD + COLOR0, road 7 TEXCOORD, unchanged). No GL limit was hit.

**Verified:** classic frames (Advanced, Day, updates 180 to 720) vs baseline: MAE 0.128, 0.087, 0.004, 0.006 (the 180 frame is in the known noise region). Integrated vs classic: shadow positions and extents of the car and rail shadows on the road match; edges are the same 10-tap character but without the blur (a little harder). Night (TIMEOFDAY=1, front camera): headlight pool on the road is identical to classic, rail shadow no longer dims lights. Cost proxies, Expert, vsync off, 3000 frames, High, tod 1.0: classic draw 1.18 ms, 380 fps, 517 draws, 1.01 M prims, 6 render target sets; integrated draw 1.12 ms, 592 fps, 454 draws, 0.67 M prims, 2 render target sets (the shadow prep mark fell from 0.254 to 0.069 ms).

(4) The Performance-Rig patch conflicts with the one-line `GameScreen` edit; revert that line, apply, then redo it. (5) One run hung at startup (exit 142 with an empty log) while another process was using the machine; a rerun was fine.

**Open:** strength/ambient floor; normal offset and slope bias (0 now); banner and sign exclusion for `Specular20`; the car, glass and `Diffuse20` objects; tunnel and time-of-day shadow colour (integrated ignores `ShadowColor`); the missing depth-buffer clear that `RenderShadows` ended with (no visible effect seen); no new rig patch was needed (`Shadow-Rig-sp1.patch` not created). ### Checkpoint 1 (2026-11-23, orchestrator): terrain and road, strength matched

- **Strength fix:** the first version scaled only the sun term, so shadows were about 1.85x brighter than classic. Shadow now multiplies **ambient plus sun** by the same `ShadowColor` the classic overlay uses (new `ShadowTint` uniform, includes the time-of-day and tunnel fading), and leaves the lamp and headlight term alone.
- **Result (golden hour, 3 tracks):** real shadows (car and guardrail shadows on the road) land in the same places as classic with matching or darker strength and crisper edges (no blur pass).
- **Finding: classic also darkens the whole road, not only shadows.** A plain road patch reads luminance 43 in classic, 68 integrated and 71 with shadows off (Beginner; Advanced 65/75/76, Expert 68/84/84). So the classic look includes an overall road darkening that is not a real shadow (probably the road's own shadow on the terrain beneath it, composited over the road). Integrated shadows do not reproduce that: the road reads lighter outside real shadows. This is the main visible difference and a look decision for the owner.
- **Cost proxies (agent, Expert, vsync off, 3000 frames, run 2 of 2):** render target sets 6 to 2, draws 517 to 454, triangles 1.01 M to 0.67 M (no receive pass re-draw), Draw CPU 1.18 to 1.12 ms, fps 380 to 592. GPU time not measured.

### SP4a (2026-11-23): natural shadow model on terrain and road. Uncommitted in worktree.

## 11. Revised goal (2026-11-23): natural shadows driven by the environment

You clarified that hard edges are not the goal: you want shadows that **look natural, as close to natural as possible, based on the environmental lighting**. Checkpoint 1 (sections 10) already showed that putting the shadow in the lighting is the right base. This section redefines what "better" means and re-orders the work.

### What natural means here

1. **Fill from the environment, not a grey multiply.** In real life a shadowed surface loses the *sun* but keeps the *sky* light. The classic overlay (and my checkpoint 1 strength fix) multiplies by a fixed colour (0.25, 0.26, 0.27). Instead: shadow = sun term removed, ambient kept, with the ambient colour and strength taken from the current environment (the time-of-day ambient and sky tints), plus a small "sky occlusion" factor (some sky is blocked near the occluder). Consequence: at golden hour the sun is warm and the shadows are cooler and bluer; at dusk the shadows are soft and low-contrast; nothing is ever pure black or neutral grey.
2. **Contact-hardening penumbra.** Real shadows are sharp where they touch the object and get softer further from it. Replace the fixed 10-tap filter with a blocker search plus a variable-width filter on the existing linear depth map (PCSS-lite): penumbra width = (receiver distance - average blocker distance) x the sun's angular size. A guardrail shadow stays crisp, the far end of a palm shadow softens. The sun's apparent size is a single tuning value; tap counts are the quality setting (fewer on Low).
3. **One sun.** Today the visible sun (lens flare) is at `DefaultSunPos` but all shading and the shadow map use `DefaultLightPos` (about 60 degrees different in azimuth, higher in the sky), so shadows do not point away from the sun you can see. For natural results the visible sun, the lighting and the shadows must share one direction. Plan: place the visible sun along the lighting/shadow direction (a small change in `TrackLighting.TimeOfDay.cs`/`LensFlare`), so the lit look of the game does not change.
4. **Time of day.** Shadows follow the same preset values: crisp and warm-lit at noon, long and softer at golden hour, faint and cool at dusk, essentially gone at night (the moon term is small, so the same code gives a faint shadow), and none in tunnels (the tunnel blend already removes the sun term).
5. **Distance behaviour.** Shadows fade smoothly with distance from the camera and at the edge of the shadow map (no visible cut-off line), instead of a texture-based fade that only approximates it.
6. **Lights stay lit** (unchanged): lamps and headlights are not dimmed by the sun's shadows.
7. **Not in scope:** shadows cast by the headlights or lamps (they would look very natural at night but cost an extra shadow map each; a later step), and a contact-shadow or ambient-occlusion blob under the car.
