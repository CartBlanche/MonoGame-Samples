# Unity FPS Microgame -> MonoGame port: plan and status

Source: `../FirstPersonShooter` (Unity 6000.6.7f1, URP 17.6). Target: this folder (Core + Platforms/Desktop, layout as SolidOps).
Goal: like-for-like look and feel (not pixel/byte identical). Scope: MainScene + IntroMenu/Win/Lose UI. SecondaryScene (level 2) is out of scope for now.

## Decisions
- Right-handed MonoGame world; Unity (x,y,z) -> (x,y,-z), quaternion (-x,-y,z,w). See `Core/Content/ASSET-CONVENTIONS.md`, `Core/Content/Characters/SKINNING-NOTES.md`.
- Linear-light rendering; sRGB textures decoded at sample time, encoded in the final pass. Textures downscaled to Unity's 512 player cap (UI and skybox full-res).
- Content pipeline extensions target net8.0 (dotnet-mgcb 3.8.5 runs on .NET 8).
- Regenerate, don't hand-edit: `Tools/ContentGen` (Shooter.mgcb, AssetMap.json), `Tools/UnityExtractor` (scene JSON), `Tools/SceneBake` (MainScene.scene.json), `Tools/Shooter.CharacterExport` (animator/skeleton JSON).

## Verification workflow (Play-mode captures -> `--shot`)
- Add `--ammo <ratio>` to draw the weapon with that ammo (gem colour, fuel cells; steam and cylinders need `--vfx 1.5` or so to have aged).
- Add `--vfx [seconds]` to spawn the Blaster muzzle flash and an impact 8 m ahead of each pose camera (aged by that many seconds, default 0.04) after the weapon has been raised.
- Add `--hud` (and `--hud-time <s>`, `--hud-target`) to draw the HUD; the shots then load the collision world because the HUD binds to the player and weapon manager.
- Reference: `Tools/UnityReference/PlayMode/game/*.png` (Unity Game view, ~1917x1081, includes HUD and the weapon). Poses: `Tools/UnityReference/PlayMode/poses.json` (Unity coordinates; `POSES.md` is the old approximate list and is superseded).
- Render: `dotnet run --project Platforms/Desktop/Shooter.csproj -- --shot <dir> --weapon --poses Tools/UnityReference/PlayMode/poses.json [--pose <name>]` (1280x720, no HUD). Each pose is rendered twice so motion blur does not smear from the previous pose.
- Reading a pose from Unity: select the Player in Play mode and read Transform Position and Rotation Y; the camera sits at root Y + 1.44 (Main Camera local Y), so world Y is 1.691 at rest. Pitch is the Main Camera local Rotation X (clamped to +-89); it differs per shot, so capture it too. Widen the Inspector so values are not clipped.
- The Game-view compass gives yaw = compass heading + 90 (validated on pm_01, pm_02, pm_03, pm_10). Handy when only a screenshot exists.
- Compare by feature positions (wall corners, seams, doorway, markers) as fractions of frame width/height, plus mean colour of a world-only region. Contrast-stretch crops to see the near-flat teal walls.
- Pose status: pm_01-06, pm_10-12 match; pm_17 is an estimate for the Turret shots (yaw from the compass, distance/offset/pitch from apparent size). pm_07-09 positions are a fit (position and yaw trade off) and their pitches are estimates. pm_13-15 are HoverBot guesses/debug views. pm_02 and pm_03 now use true 90/180 degree turns from the start.

## Findings that affect later phases
- Play-mode player settles to root Y 0.2509, Z -7.149 (baked start is Y -0.2443, Z -7.25 in Unity coordinates, i.e. editor values before gravity).
- Enemy transforms in the scene JSON are the editor pose (identity rotation). In Play mode the HoverBot is at rotation Y -89.28 (Unity); `SceneLoader.AddStaticEnemies` applies +89.28 about Y in MonoGame space as a stand-in until patrol/orientation logic exists.
- The animator's default state `Idle` plays `HoverBot@IdleClosed`, a loop (1.93 s) in which the front panel opens around 0.35-0.5 s and shows the eye. The static preview samples it at 0.42 s.
- Eye glow: `EnemyController` writes `DefaultEyeColor` (23.85, 7.29, 0) into `_EmissionColor` through a MaterialPropertyBlock and Unity treats it as gamma -> linear (about 1800, 105, 0). The material asset's value is not what is drawn. `AttackEyeColor` (32, 1.46, 0) is used once alerted. The Turret eye uses the same mechanism.
- Blaster emission map (`Blaster_Emissive`) must be sampled without the sRGB decode to match Unity; other emission maps are untested (`SceneLoader.BuildMaterial`).
- Material part tags (`Material`, `Material.015`) in the FBX models do not match Unity material names; the loader falls back to submesh order, which is correct for the blaster and HoverBot.

## Phase 4a: player controller and collision
- Code: `Core/Physics/CollisionWorld.cs` (triangle soup + uniform grid, capsule overlap/cast), `Core/Physics/CharacterMotor.cs` (CharacterController stand-in), `Core/Gameplay/PlayerController.cs` (line-by-line port of `PlayerCharacterController.Update`), `PlayerInput.cs` (PlayerInputHandler + bindings from `InputSystem_Actions.inputactions`), `PlayerSettings.cs` (Inspector values read from the scene JSON, with the script defaults as fallback), `PhysicsSelfTest.cs`. Hooked into `SceneLoader.BuildCollision` and `ShooterGame` (`--play`, F2, `--test-physics`).
- Collider representation: all 1,966 MeshColliders are non-convex and reuse the mesh the renderer draws, so each is expanded to world-space triangles (two-sided, 94,924 in total) from the same Model buffers and instance matrices; boxes (3) and the sphere (1) become triangles too (enemy hitboxes, static for now); the 2 trigger boxes (pickups) and the player's own capsule are skipped. A capsule is tested against triangles with exact segment-triangle distance. PhysX is not available, so `CharacterMotor.Move` is sub-stepped (<= 0.08 m, radius/4, so zero-thickness walls cannot be skipped) with overlap recovery after each sub-step, which gives wall sliding; plus step offset (lift, move, drop, only from walkable ground onto walkable ground), slope limit (up-facing surfaces steeper than the limit push the capsule off sideways and a grounded move cannot be carried up them by the projected velocity) and skin width.
- Values come from the scene: MaxSpeedOnGround 13, sprint x1.5, crouch x0.5, gravity 20, jump 9, heights 1.8/0.9, radius 0.35, slope 45, step 0.3, skin 0.001, kill -50, camera 0.8 x height (= 1.44 standing). Note GroundCheckDistance is 1 in the scene (script default 0.05), so a grounded player snaps down ledges up to 1 m.
- Camera: yaw is kept in Unity degrees (positive = right), pitch in Unity camera-local X degrees (positive = down, clamped +-89), so Inspector reads can be typed in directly; `CameraWorld` equals `UnityConvert.CameraWorldFromUnityPose` (checked).
- Verified (`--test-physics`): settle on floor; wall stop at 60/30/10 fps, walk and sprint, against a zero-thickness wall (x = 5 - 0.35 - skin, never beyond); 45 degree wall slide; speeds 13 / 19.5 / 6.5; step 0.2 and 0.3 climbed, 0.35 blocked; jump apex 2.10 m (ideal 2.025) with landing; 30 degree ramp climbed, 60 degree ramp not; cannot stand or sprint-stand under a 1.2 m ceiling; kill height; 10 m fall lands at 20.0 m/s; real level: spawn settles at (-28.489, 0.251, 7.149) vs Unity Play mode (-28.5, 0.2509, 7.149); sprint 10 s in 8 headings and 3 x 4 random-input fuzz runs (60/30/15 fps, jumps and crouches, 90 s each) never overlap geometry by more than 5 cm and never get out of the level while grounded; cost 0.35-1.3 ms per frame (debug build).
- Spawn: the baked start (Y -0.244) is inside a 0.5 m floor slab (floor top is y = 0.25). PhysX recovery moved it +0.495 up and 0.101 along z (off the neighbouring cell's internal face at z = 7.5); `CharacterMotor.Recover()` resolves the shallowest contact first, which reproduces that (Z 7.149, Y 0.251). This ordering is calibrated on that single observation (X differs by 1 cm); normal movement recovers deepest-first.
- Not verified / known differences: no side-by-side feel test against Unity (acceleration, step and slope behaviour of PhysX were reproduced from its documented rules, not compared); no moving platforms or enemy collision (enemy hitboxes are static); gamepad and look acceleration not ported; fall damage is computed and exposed (`Landed` event) but there is no Health yet; death only sets `IsDead`; play mode respawns 1.5 s later as a stand-in for the Lose screen / Play Again (UI pass), R respawns at once; `Died`, `Jumped`, `Landed` events have no audio/UI consumers yet; capsule casts are sampled sweeps (step 0.0875 m + bisection), not analytic.

## Phase 4b: weapons
- Code: `Core/Gameplay/Weapons/` - `WeaponController` (ammo as a refilling resource, 0.1 s fire gate, manual/automatic/charge), `ProjectileStandard` + `ProjectileManager` (speed, camera trajectory correction, camera-to-muzzle wall check, hit cast from last root to tip), `PlayerWeaponsManager` (slots, put-up/put-down, aim, bob, recoil, FOV). `CollisionWorld.Raycast` (grid walk + Moller-Trumbore) serves the casts. Fire = left mouse, aim = right mouse, wheel / E / Q / 1-9 switch (one weapon only, so switching is a no-op), mouse look is x0.4 while aiming. R is respawn, not reload (the Blaster reloads itself).
- Everything comes from the data: `SceneBake` now also emits `weapon.aimingInCamera`, `weapon.downInCamera` and a `projectile` section (Projectile_Blaster pieces, tip, ProjectileStandard fields); the scene's own values are used, not the script defaults: WeaponSwitchDelay 0.2 (not 1), MaxRecoilDistance 0.2 (not 0.5), AimingAnimationSpeed 14 (not 10), Blaster delay 0.1, 16 ammo refilling 16/s after 1 s, aim zoom 0.5 (FOV 60 -> 30), projectile speed 300, damage 15, correction distance 5.
- The weapon logic keeps Unity camera-local coordinates (z forward) internally and converts at the edge, so the formulas are copies of the script. The viewmodel is the baked pieces placed at camera + `SocketLocal` (bob + recoil + aim + switch), rendered with the zoomed FOV on both cameras.
- The prefab's projectile Tip sits 10 m in front of its Root (mesh scaled x10 in z), so hits are detected up to 10 m ahead of the projectile and the 5 m trajectory correction has not finished for targets closer than about 15 m; that is how the prefab is authored, and it is reproduced.
- Verified (`--test-physics`, "Weapons"): scene values read; raise Down -> Default; 9 shots in the first second (7 frame gap = 0.1167 s) with ammo 16 -> 7, 16 shots in 2.5 s of held fire; refill starts 1 s after the last shot at 16/s; recoil peaks at 0.1995 of 0.2 and returns; aim FOV 30 / socket / look x0.4 and back; bob 0.0333 walking and 0.05 sprinting (0.05 x speed/19.5); a shot hits the screen centre 20 m away within 1 mm; a wall closer than the muzzle is hit at once; the switch state machine; on the real level 24 of 24 shots stop where an independent camera ray hits (worst 3.6 mm).
- Not done: muzzle flash, impact VFX and weapon audio (the `ShotFired` / `ProjectileManager.Impact` hooks exist), crosshair and ammo HUD, `OverheatBehavior` (gem colour and steam with ammo) and `WeaponFuelCellHandler` (fuel cells slide as ammo drops), `IsPointingAtEnemy`, damage (no Health / Damageable yet, `ProjectileImpact.Damage` is carried), the sphere cast is a ray (1 cm radius ignored), manual reload and physical shells (shotgun, SecondaryScene), weapon pickups. The shot-harness option `--projectile` draws a projectile 8 m ahead of each pose for visual checks.

## Phase 4c-2: in-game HUD
- Code: `Core/UI/Hud.cs` (layout, state, drawing), `PlayerWeaponsManager.IsPointingAtEnemy` (ray from the camera through `CollisionWorld.Raycast`; `IsEnemyHit` is a stub that says no until enemies exist), crosshair / icon data in `WeaponSettings` (`CrosshairData`, `WeaponIcon`, read from the WeaponController fields), `FinalPost.fx` + `PostProcessor.Overlay` (HUD composite), `--hud*` options in `ShooterGame`.
- Source of the layout: `GameHUD.prefab` instance under GameManager in `Tools/UnityExtractor/out/Scenes/MainScene.json` plus the AmmoCounter, GameMessageGeneric, CompassMarkerDirection and ObjectiveToastPrimary prefabs. CanvasScaler: scale with screen size, reference 960 x 540, match width, so canvas px = screen width / 960. RectTransforms are resolved by `Hud.Layout` (anchors, anchoredPosition, sizeDelta, pivot), kept in Unity's y-up canvas space.
- Crosshair: `CrosshairManager.CrosshairImage` is the `V` image, not the parent: the weapon's `CrosshairDataDefault` (Crosshair_Hexa, size 55, white alpha 0.384) replaces the scene's Crosshair_Blaster_Center sprite; `TargetInSight` is red, size 40. Size and colour lerp at sharpness 5, the image keeps the sprite aspect. The "+" in the middle is part of the Hexa sprite.
- Ammo bar: AmmoCounter 153 x 22 at the bottom right (padding 25), fill = `CurrentAmmoRatio` lerped at 20, weapon icon black on top, FillBarColorChange flashes the fill black when the bar becomes full and the background red below one shot's worth (1/16). Index / reload / control-key parts are hidden for a single weapon without physical bullets.
- Also: health bar (194 x 30, fill ratio 1 until Health exists), stance box with the stand / crouch icon, compass (388 x 30, scissor-clipped, direction ticks scale 0.5 because Compass.Update gives directions a distance ratio of 1, label size 22 x 0.5), Tab hint, the objective message (`ObjectiveKillEnemies.Title`, broadcast at start: fade 0.5 / 3 s / 0.5 on a 0.7-scaled band from Gradiants.png) and the objective toast (ObjectiveToastPrimary, appears after `DelayVisible` 4 s, orange upper-case title, description strip, no counter text yet).
- Fonts are the pre-generated Roboto spritefonts (7, 9, 10, 12, 22, 35). A spritefont's size is in points, so text is drawn at 0.75 x (Unity size / nominal size) to match TextMeshPro pixel sizes (`Hud.FontPixelFactor`, checked on the message width).
- UI blending: Unity blends the UI over the finished frame in linear light (measured: black alpha 0.6 over 32 gives 18, a gamma blend gives 13; white alpha 0.1 over the sky gives G 109 vs 82). The HUD is therefore drawn into an off-screen layer with premultiplied accumulation and linear tints, and `FinalPost.fx` blends it after FXAA / grain, right before the sRGB encode. `ISceneRenderer.Overlay` carries it; the Basic diagnostics renderer ignores it.
- Verified: positions and text boxes against `pm_01_start` (compass N / W letters, health, ammo, Tab, message, crosshair) within 1-2 px at 1280x720; objective toast against `pm_04_turn270` (hud-time 8); target-in-sight crosshair rendered (red, smaller); translucent overlays within ~5 levels of the reference; `--test-physics` now has 44 checks (crosshair / icon data, IsPointingAtEnemy with a stub enemy test, ammo-needed ratio).
- Known differences: (1) Unity shows opaque saturated UI colours desaturated and shifted (pure red (255,0,0) -> (234,51,35), health fill (249,33,55) -> (229,61,65), orange (255,179,0) -> (239,179,62); white stays 255); the cause is not identified (not the linear blend), so the colours are drawn as authored. (2) The reference compass for `pm_02_turn90` and `pm_03_turn180` reads yaw about 185.6 and 287 (using the verified N/E/S/W formula that matches pm_01, pm_04, pm_05, pm_06 within 3 px), whereas `poses.json` has 169.5 and 256.2 for them, so those two poses are probably off in yaw; not changed. (3) Play mode logs one OpenGL driver warning ("unit 0 ... unloadable") in the first frames once the HUD layer is attached; skipping the layer for the first 30 frames removes it, the cause was not found in 3 tries (layer allocation, texture unbinding and PreserveContents did not help), and the headless shots are unaffected. (4) The compass enemy markers (red dots in the reference), jetpack bar (no jetpack pickup), notification toasts, objective counter, damage flash and the low-health vignette are not drawn: they need enemies, Health and pickups. (5) Window resizing is not handled beyond reallocating the layer; the window is fixed at 1280 x 720.

## Phase 4c-1b: overheat gem, steam, fuel cells
